# 🎤 Speaker Script (10-15 minutes total)

*This script is divided into two parts. **Speaker 1** will present the introduction, dataset, UNet, and final quantitative results. **Speaker 2** will present the mathematical approaches (TV), generative approaches (DiffPIR), and the visual trade-offs. Each slide takes about 45-60 seconds to explain.*

---

## Part 1: Speaker 1 (Approx. 5-6 minutes)

### Slide 1: Title
**Speaker 1:** 
"Good morning everyone. Today, my colleague and I are presenting our Computational Imaging project. We tackled the inverse problem of Super-Resolution using three fundamentally different methodological families: End-to-End, Variational, and Generative. Our goal was to compare their theoretical trade-offs on a natural image dataset."

### Slide 2: The Inverse Problem
**Speaker 1:**
"Our task is Super-Resolution: reconstructing high-quality RGB images from degraded, low-resolution observations. We formulated the forward model mathematically as $y = Hx + e$. The operator $H$ represents a Gaussian blur followed by downsampling by a factor of 2, and $e$ represents additive white Gaussian noise with a standard deviation of 0.005."

### Slide 3: Dataset & Setup
**Speaker 1:**
"To ensure a fair and deterministic comparison, we built a unified degradation pipeline. We used a subset of 4000 RGB images from the LSUN Church dataset, natively sized at 256x256 pixels. All three methods—UNet, TV, and DiffPIR—were evaluated on the exact same noisy inputs generated by our pipeline."

### Slide 4: Method 1 - End-to-End Learning (UNet)
**Speaker 1:**
"The first approach we implemented is an End-to-End deep learning method. We built a custom UNet architecture and trained it from scratch on our Kaggle environment using dual GPUs. The network was optimized using an L1 Loss function over 50 epochs."

### Slide 5: UNet - Theoretical Behavior
**Speaker 1:**
"From a theoretical standpoint, minimizing an L1 or L2 loss forces the network to predict the conditional mean of all possible high-resolution patches. Because of this 'regression to the mean', the network is incredibly effective at removing noise. However, it struggles to synthesize complex high-frequency details, resulting in slightly over-smoothed textures like blurry leaves or bricks."

---

## Part 2: Speaker 2 (Approx. 5-6 minutes)

### Slide 6: Method 2 - Variational (Total Variation)
**Speaker 2:**
"Thank you. The second approach we implemented is a classical Variational method using Total Variation regularization, solved via FISTA. Since standard TV algorithms are formulated for grayscale images, we adapted the mathematical operators to process 3D RGB tensors by computing the divergence channel-by-channel. We heuristically set the regularization weight lambda to 0.05 to balance data fidelity and smoothness."

### Slide 7: TV - Theoretical Behavior
**Speaker 2:**
"The main theoretical assumption of Total Variation is that images are 'piecewise constant'. While this is excellent for medical imaging like CT scans—where organs and bones have uniform colors—it is problematic for natural images. As a result, our TV reconstruction preserves sharp edges but creates a strong 'staircase' or 'watercolor' effect, completely destroying natural textures like the sky or trees."

### Slide 8: Method 3 - Generative (DiffPIR)
**Speaker 2:**
"Our third and final approach is DiffPIR, which uses a pre-trained unconditional diffusion model as a generative prior. At each reverse diffusion step, it blends the generative denoising process with a gradient step that enforces data consistency with our specific blurred observation."

### Slide 9: DiffPIR - Theoretical Behavior
**Speaker 2:**
"Unlike the UNet, DiffPIR does not predict an average image; it samples from the actual data distribution. This allows it to 'hallucinate' highly realistic, high-frequency details. However, balancing the generative prior with the data-consistency constraint can sometimes introduce minor generative noise or grid-like artifacts, which we can observe in some sky regions."

---

## Part 3: Wrapping Up (Speaker 1 & 2)

### Slide 10: Quantitative Results (Speaker 1)
**Speaker 1:**
"We evaluated our methods on 50 test images. Looking at the quantitative results, the UNet heavily dominates the metrics, achieving a PSNR of 27.5 dB and an SSIM of 0.87. Interestingly, Total Variation performed worse (24.3 dB), and DiffPIR scored the lowest mathematically (23.3 dB)."

### Slide 11: Qualitative Comparison (Speaker 2)
**Speaker 2:**
"However, these metrics only tell half the story. As you can see in this visual comparison, DiffPIR (on the far right) generates incredibly sharp and realistic textures that the UNet completely smoothed out. This perfectly demonstrates the 'Perception-Distortion Trade-off' theorem we studied in class: DiffPIR sacrifices pixel-by-pixel mathematical accuracy (PSNR) to achieve much higher perceptual quality, while UNet does the exact opposite."

### Slide 12: Conclusions (Speaker 1)
**Speaker 1:**
"To conclude, our comparative analysis confirms that there is no perfect method. End-to-end models like UNet are best for pure mathematical accuracy and fast inference. Variational methods struggle with natural textures but offer solid mathematical guarantees. Finally, Generative models provide state-of-the-art visual realism, but at the cost of slow inference and lower pixel-wise fidelity. Thank you for your attention!"
